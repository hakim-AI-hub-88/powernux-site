Voice typing on Linux: dictate anywhere with Whisper, Groq or AssemblyAI

By Es-somid Abdelhakim 6 min readNovember 27, 2026

Typing long messages, commit descriptions or emails is faster by voice — if dictation is one keystroke away and works in every application. That is what Powernux Voice Typing does on Linux.

Hold, speak, release

  1. Hold Win + E: a notch slides down from the top of the screen with live, colored sound waves.
  2. Speak naturally.
  3. Release: the audio is transcribed, copied to your clipboard and pasted automatically where your cursor is.

A short press switches to hands-free mode: keep talking, press the shortcut again to finish.

Choose your transcription engine

  • Groq — Whisper large v3 turbo, extremely fast, with a free tier.
  • OpenAI — gpt-4o-mini-transcribe.
  • AssemblyAI — Universal speech model, automatic language detection.
  • Your own server — any OpenAI-compatible or AssemblyAI-compatible API, from the advanced developer mode (great for a local Whisper server).

Privacy

Audio is recorded only while you hold the shortcut, sent to the service you selected, and deleted right after transcription. API keys live in the GNOME Keyring.

Paste anywhere — even in terminals

Voice Typing simulates Ctrl + V through GNOME Shell, which works on Wayland. For terminals, switch the paste keys to Ctrl + Shift + V in the settings.

Install Powernux, then add Voice Typing from Tools Manager and paste your API key.
